## 1. What we're building

**The insight:** Language Soup is **social media**, not EdTech. "Social media where the activity is speaking a new language." "A dinner party, not a classroom."

**Not competing with:** Duolingo (game), Babbel (classroom), content apps with high churn.  
**Competing with:** Nothing. First of its kind.

**Timeline:** Dec 2024 research → July 2025 full-time → Oct 2025 WhatsApp (100 users) → Dec 2025 building app → Jan 6, 2026 launch → today: retention focus.

**Product philosophy:** Learn by speaking. Unstructured immersion. Real connection (not addiction mechanics). Ethical monetization (creator economy later). Social-first.

**Business model:** Discovering, not fixed. Probably creator economy (platform free, creators charge). Not premium subscriptions. Experiments: Journal (failed) → WhatsApp (worked) → App (working) → ??? (creator economy).

**Current focus:** Retention (Day 0–7), daily habit, network effects. NOT monetization yet.

**Unfair advantages:** Community builder, language learner (Fulbright, Hungarian), creator (journal, TikTok), builder (54k LOC, 8 weeks).

**What to remember:** Noah is in **Social** (network effects, creators, habits), not EdTech (curriculum, lessons). Success = retention + word of mouth.

## 2. Why Noah builds this way (Fulbright research)

Noah studied **why Hungarian startups fail at pre-seed** (49% stuck; perfectionism, no customer iteration, can't sell). He built Language Soup to avoid those failure modes.

**Grounded theory → product:** Users define the product. "We're discovering the business model." He's responding to user data, not guessing.

**Avoiding perfectionism:** Journal failed → pivoted. WhatsApp validated before app. Shipped app in 6–8 weeks. Retention over vanity metrics.

**User-obsessed:** WhatsApp before building. Daily Challenges from interviews. Measures retention, not downloads.

**Social-first, not EdTech-first:** "I'm in social media. Dinner party, not classroom." Build for **speaking with each other**, not teaching grammar.

**Bootstrapped by design:** Infinite runway, no fundraising until retention. Build for users, not investors.

**Multilingual from day 1:** 13 languages; global social language app, not "Hungarian app."

**For Cursor:** He's not winging it. Speed > perfection. Retention is the metric. Social > EdTech. Discover, don't assume.

## 4. How to say it (and not sound lame)

**Cool line:** **"Language social media."** Social media where the activity is speaking a new language.

**The nuance:** It's **not** classic social media (doom-scroll, likes, performative). It's **not** EdTech (classroom, curriculum, homework). It's **in between**: **community learning** — learning with real people, by speaking, low-pressure. "Community learning" can sound lame, so we **lead with "language social media"** and clarify: real people, real speaking, no classroom vibe.

**For people who are anti–social media (including Noah and many friends):** Small groups, voice-first, no feed to perform on. "Like a dinner party" = you're with a few people, talking. So it's **social** (you're with others) but not "social media" in the addictive, broadcast sense. It's **learning together**, not learning alone — and that doesn't have to sound cheesy if we say it as "language social media" and show it (small groups, voice, community).

**One-liners we can use:** "Language social media." "Speak with real people, not a textbook." "A dinner party, not a classroom." "Learning with others, not alone."

## 5. What this means for the app

**Copy & positioning**
- Use: **speak**, **your people**, **community**, **with others**, **real people**.
- Avoid: **lesson**, **course**, **study**, **homework**, **level up**, **complete module** (unless we're being playful).
- Taglines and empty states: we're about **speaking with real people**, not completing a curriculum.

**UX emphasis**
- Surface **who replied**, **your groups**, **voices** — the social bits. Retention = coming back to **people**, not only to a streak.
- Streaks and progress are fine but should feel like "you showed up for your people" not "you did your lesson."

**Tone**
- Fun, low-pressure, "dinner party" not "classroom." Already in product rules; keep reinforcing.
- When we add features, ask: **Does this make users want to speak with each other?** Not "Does this teach grammar better?"

**Summary for Cursor:** We're building **language social media** — community learning that doesn't sound lame. Lead with "speak with real people"; avoid EdTech language. Social = connection and speaking together; not addiction mechanics.
