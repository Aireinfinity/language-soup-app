# Today tab — bold concept: one moment, one focus

**Goal:** When you open the tab, you feel **excited** and you know **exactly where to look**. One thing grabs your eye. Nothing else competes.

---

## The problem with "balanced"

When everything has similar weight (header strip, timeline, hero, next section, who replied, your groups), nothing wins. The eye wanders. It feels busy and a bit boring because there's no clear moment.

---

## Bold direction: one stage, everything else support

**One focal point:** The challenge block is the **stage**. It's the only thing that gets full color, size, and energy. Everything else is **supporting** — lighter, smaller, or clearly secondary.

- **Above the hero:** Minimal. One line: "today" + one status word (or a tiny pill). No timeline row of three boxes — or timeline becomes a single compact line (e.g. "today: [prompt snippet]" only, or yesterday · today · tomorrow as small text). The idea: you scroll a tiny bit or look down and **boom** — the challenge is the only thing that shouts.

- **The hero:** Full-bleed color, bowls, big type, one CTA. It should feel like "this is the main event." No shrinking it to fit the rest of the page.

- **Below the hero:** More **whitespace**. "Next challenge," "who replied," "your groups" are **calm** — same cream/white as the page, no tinted cards competing for attention. Section titles are small and lowercase. Content is there when you need it but doesn't grab your eye. So after the hero, the page "exhales" instead of crowding.

---

## Excitement = contrast + one punch

- **Contrast:** One big, bold, colored moment (the hero) on a calm background (cream). Nothing else uses strong color except maybe one small accent (e.g. "do another" button).
- **One punch:** One headline or one line that says the mood: "your turn" or "today's challenge" or "let's go" — big, bold, at the top of the hero or right above it. So the eye goes: **that line → hero → tap.**

---

## Less crowded = more air

- **More padding** between sections (e.g. 28–32px instead of 18–20).
- **Fewer elements** in the header: just "today" + status (no pill if it clutters; or one small pill).
- **Timeline:** Either remove it from the main view or make it a single line under the header (e.g. "today: [one prompt snippet]" with yesterday/tomorrow as subtle links or one line of small text). So we don't have three equal boxes fighting the hero.
- **Sections below:** One title, then content. No extra borders or heavy cards — soft background or none, so the hero remains the only "card" that pops.

---

## Summary

- **One stage:** The challenge block is the only thing that's bold and colorful.
- **One line:** One clear headline or status above/near the hero ("your turn," "today's challenge," "let's go").
- **Support, don't compete:** Header and timeline are minimal. Sections below are calm (cream/white, lots of space, small labels).
- **Excitement:** Contrast (one punch of color + type) + whitespace (room to breathe) so the tab feels exciting, not busy.

Use this to drive the next visual pass: strip back everything that isn't the hero, add air, and make the hero unmissable.
